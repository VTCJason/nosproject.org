## What is this?

The **nOS Manifesto** is a declaration that AI is not a product update — it is a civilizational shift in how computing works, who holds platform power, and what it means to do work.

The central argument: every major computing era has migrated platform power up the architectural stack (mainframe → PC → OS → browser → cloud → mobile). That migration is happening again. AI is dissolving the operating system as the user's primary surface, replacing it with an intent-driven, autonomous compute layer — what this project calls the **nOS** (non-operating-system architecture).

The site is built around five sections:

- **The Manifesto** — the core declaration, written for builders, buyers, regulators, and educators
- **Case Study: When the Tenant Becomes the Landlord** — empirical analysis of how AI reshapes leverage across the semiconductor-to-software stack, with an interactive exhibit
- **The Human Question** — a structured intelligence dossier across 10 domains: economics & labor, ethics & society, governance, geopolitics, existential risk, and more (~51,000 words)
- **Essays** — long-form analysis on specific AI-era decisions and moments
- **Glossary** — a working vocabulary for the transition

---

## Why does it exist?

Most public discourse about AI is either breathless enthusiasm or abstract alarm. Neither gives practitioners, buyers, or policymakers a working framework for the decisions in front of them *right now*.

This project attempts something more disciplined: name the migration that is already underway, map its structure across industries and institutions, surface the hardest questions honestly (including the ones without good answers), and build a shared vocabulary precise enough to act on.

The manifesto is signed. The analysis is open. The goal is a public record that serious people can cite, argue with, and build on.
